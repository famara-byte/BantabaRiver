About Bantaba

A river company rooted in The Gambia

Bantaba River & Eco-Tourism is an eco-tourism company based in Senegambia, Kanifing (KMC), The Gambia. We run river tours and nature and culture experiences on and around the River Gambia — the waterway that shapes the country’s landscape, wildlife and daily life.

Our name comes from the bantaba — the gathering place under the tree where a community meets, talks and welcomes visitors. That is the spirit we bring to the river: unhurried, welcoming, and connected to the people and places along its banks.

Every experience is designed around sustainable eco-tourism: small, respectful encounters with nature and culture that give visitors a real sense of The Gambia, while supporting a greener, more beautiful Gambia for the communities who live here.

On the river · Signature experience

River Cruises

Drift the wide, calm waters of the River Gambia by boat, past mangrove banks, fishing activity and riverside settlements. A river cruise is the most relaxed way to take in the scale of the river — its light, its birdlife and the life that moves along it every day.

  • Unhurried cruising on the main river and its quieter stretches
  • Mangrove-lined banks, open skies and ever-changing river light
  • A good first experience if you’re new to The Gambia

Birdwatching

The riverbanks, creeks and wetlands around the River Gambia are rich birding country. Head out by boat and on foot to watch for the species that feed, nest and rest along the water — best enjoyed slowly, with time to stop, look and listen.

Nature & wildlife · Day trip

Fathala Wildlife Reserve

Just across the border in Senegal’s Sine-Saloum region, Fathala is the closest safari to The Gambia. Head out on a guided drive through its woodland and grassland in search of the reserve’s stars — its lions — along with giraffes, rhinos, antelopes and warthogs. A big-wildlife day trip, arranged from the coast.

Culture · Banjul

Albert Market, Banjul

In the heart of the capital, under the famous yellow “Welcome to Royal Albert Market” arch, Banjul’s main market has traded since the 19th century. Wander stalls piled with colourful fabrics, wood carvings, jewellery, spices and fresh produce — and feel the everyday energy of the city, with your guide on hand to help you browse and bargain.

Every Bantaba experience brings those three together — the natural world of the river, the culture of the people who live along it, and a way of travelling that keeps both thriving.

Sustainability · Why Bantaba

Explore • Experience • Preserve

Those three words are our working method. They describe what you do on a Bantaba experience — and what your visit helps to do in return.

01

ExploreThe river, properly

We take you beyond the viewpoint — onto the water, into the creeks and ashore in the villages — so you understand the River Gambia as a living system, not just a backdrop. Exploration is guided, unhurried and curious.

02

ExperienceNature & culture, up close

Birdsong in the mangroves, gold light on evening water, conversation under the bantaba tree. We design for real encounters — with wildlife, with place and with people — rather than passing scenery.

03

PreserveA greener, more beautiful Gambia

Mangroves protect the banks, shelter wildlife and support the communities who depend on the river. Travelling gently — respecting wildlife, places and local life — helps keep the river healthy for the next visitor and the next generation.

How we travel gently

On every experience, we ask guests to travel in a way that protects what they came to see:

  • Observe wildlife from a respectful distance — never feed or chase it
  • Take litter back with you; leave banks and villages as you found them
  • Ask before photographing people, and follow your guide’s lead in villages
  • Keep voices low in the mangroves and birding areas

Why the mangroves matter

The green fringe along the River Gambia is far more than scenery. Mangrove roots shelter young fish, their canopy feeds and houses birdlife, and their dense banks hold the shoreline together against the water.

When you explore the mangroves with us, you see that whole chain at work — and why sustainable tourism has a stake in keeping it intact.
